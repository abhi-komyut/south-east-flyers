## Positioning
- SEF is a local flyer distribution and letterbox drop operator for Melbourne's south-east and eastern suburbs.
- What we sell is certainty: your flyer goes in by itself, and you see exactly where it went.
- Make that concrete with real steps: call or send the form, start from 1,000 flyers, walkers and e-bike riders deliver, No Junk Mail respected, GPS map and drop summary sent after the run.
- Show certainty through these facts, not adjectives like "reliable", "trusted" or "hassle-free".

## Proof points (confirmed)
- Exclusive delivery: never bundled with other advertisers' flyers.
- Every route GPS-tracked in Strava; the client gets the map and a drop summary.
- Delivered on foot and by e-bike riders.
- No Junk Mail signs always respected.
- Start from 1,000 flyers.
- 48-hour start available; quote with a quick turnaround.
- We distribute, we don't print.
- Price: "from $180 per 1,000 flyers*" on the homepage stat strip and `/letterbox-drop-cost` only.

## Who we're talking to
- Main reader: the local business owner (tradie, real estate agent, café owner, dentist, groomer). Write "you" to them.
- Secondary: marketing managers and office managers at real estate agencies booking regular campaigns. Give them one line and one FAQ ("Can you run regular drops for our agency?"), not a whole section.
- Name the people: business owners, agents, tradies, practice managers. Never "customers", "users" or "visitors".

## Rules for website copy
- Professional and plain: warm but never chatty. No empathy lines ("We know marketing is hard").
- Write as "we" to "you". Sentences under 20 words, one idea each. Paragraphs up to 3 sentences.
- Australian English: organise, colour, neighbour, enquiry.
- Say each point once per page. Don't repeat "never bundled", "GPS-tracked" or "start from 1,000 flyers" in every section. Vary the wording between pages.
- For on-ground actions (walking, delivering, skipping No Junk Mail boxes), the subject is "our walkers" or "our riders", not "we".
- Plain verbs: deliver, cover, track, share, send, check, organise.
- Always invite the reader to call 0402 824 070 or send the quote form. Be persuasive, not passive: "Send us your suburb and flyer count, and we'll organise the run." Never "Let us know if you'd like to book."
- At most one question hook per page; no rhetorical questions in headings.
- Never use: minimum order, bundle deals, "guaranteed results", best, No.1, cheapest, category exclusivity, "24 hours", "5 minutes", "near me" (outside the rules in the page-build context), Sydney, em dashes, exclamation marks, and the banned vocabulary in the page-build context (7.5).
- No made-up facts. No response rates, client results, testimonials or campaign examples unless Abhi confirms them. No "most clients" or "usually" claims. If unknown, leave it out.
- No prices outside the homepage stat strip and the cost page. No per-suburb dollar maths.

## ABS census figures (allowed, Abhi, 9 Nov 2026)
This overrides the "No census statistics" rule in section 2 of the page-build context.
- Source: ABS 2021 Census QuickStats for that suburb (SAL level). Link the suburb's QuickStats page once per page, e.g. "ABS 2021 Census".
- Use only figures copied from QuickStats. Round them ("about 10,500 dwellings", "around 7 in 10 homes are separate houses"). Never estimate or blend figures.
- 2 to 3 figures per page, only where they change the targeting: dwelling count, houses vs apartments, owners vs renters, families with children.
- Tie each figure to a letterbox decision: "Most homes are separate houses, so one walker covers every letterbox on the street."
- Never use census figures to compute a price.

## Headings, meta and the text under the H1
- Title and H1: follow the page-build context format; don't change live ones without a reason.
- Text under the H1, 2 to 3 short sentences: the keyword line with the suburb or industry, one proof point (never bundled, or GPS map), then the call to action. Vary the wording between pages.
  - Example (Brighton): "Flyer distribution in Brighton, delivered on its own to every letterbox on your streets. Each run is GPS-tracked, so you see the map. Call 0402 824 070 for a quote."
- H2s: plain and functional, a keyword or an honest description. No slogans, no clever phrasing. FAQ heading: "Frequently Asked Questions".
- Meta: 140 to 155 characters. Keyword first, one proof point, then the call to action.

## Keywords: tracking and no stuffing
- Keep every primary keyword, secondary keyword and key phrase on each page when rewriting.
- Keep a tracking table per page: keyword, before count, after count, locations (title, meta, H1, H2, body, FAQ). Counts must be exact. A page fails if any keyword drops to 0.
- Primary keyword in the title, H1, meta and first 100 words, plus 2 to 4 times in the body.
- Each secondary keyword 1 to 2 times. One keyword per sentence, never more than two.
- No keyword-only sentences or FAQs. Keyword density under 2%.

## Abhi's edits on the Camberwell page (9 Nov 2026, PR #13): the model for all suburb pages
These came from Abhi rewriting the Camberwell page section by section. Treat them as the voice reference until real client chats are added.

**Lines Abhi wrote or approved:**
- Hero lead: "Get your flyers into letterboxes around Camberwell Junction and across 3124 with GPS-tracked flyer distribution."
- "A targeted letterbox campaign across Camberwell can reach up to 9,000 homes."
- "Camberwell is a strong area for real estate agents running appraisal campaigns, and lucrative for roofers working on its established homes."
- "Every drop is exclusive, with no other advertisers' flyers in the run. We also cover Hawthorn, Kew and Glen Iris, so campaigns can be spread across different suburbs."

**What Abhi pushed back on (don't do these):**
- Writing "extra": long explanations of why people buy. Say who it suits and stop.
- Repeating a fact or place in the same paragraph ("Camberwell station … Camberwell Market"). Say "the station and the market".
- Repeating the same number in several sections. State the home count once, as the reach a campaign gets ("can reach up to 9,000 homes").
- Stock phrases: "reach every home in the suburb", "considered decisions", "school families", "facilities". Use words a person would say.
- Sections with no concrete point for a flyer buyer: cut them or turn them into "who it suits / how to start".

**Patterns that work:**
- Hero lead: "Get your flyers into letterboxes around {landmark} and across {postcode} with GPS-tracked flyer distribution."
- Frame home counts and local facts as opportunity: who does well here, and how to start.
- H2s: plain and human, with the keyword in one or two ("Flyer distribution around Camberwell Junction", "Letterbox drops for Camberwell agents and trades", "Families near Camberwell's schools").
- FAQs: answer first, one or two short sentences.
- Check local facts (e.g. Camberwell Grammar is in Canterbury, not Camberwell).
